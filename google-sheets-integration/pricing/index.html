What counts as a transfer?

A primary record on an execution

A transferred contact, opportunity, invoice, or other supported primary record is a unit. Moving the same record in a new execution creates another unit.

Fields and supporting details are bundled

Selected fields, supporting lookup labels, and embedded invoice/order line items do not each become separate transfer units.

Retries need the original operation identity

A retry of the same delivery must not create duplicate charges. A genuinely new execution transferring records creates new usage.

Reports use the data already delivered

A chart recalculation from existing worksheet data is not another provider transfer. A source refresh that transfers records adds usage.

Pricing questions

Is the price per record per month?

No. The billable unit is a successfully transferred primary record on each execution. The monthly period applies to the free allowance and volume-discount accumulation.

Do repeated refreshes count again?

Yes, when the execution transfers primary records again. A report recalculation from already delivered worksheet data is different from a source refresh.

Do I pay for every invoice line?

Embedded invoice lines are bundled with their primary invoice. The same parent rule applies to embedded order lines; standalone primary datasets have their own record identities.

Does a failed transfer count?

Planned metering counts successfully delivered primary records. The same delivery operation must not be charged twice when retried or reconciled.

Can an agency negotiate a subscription?

Yes. Talk to Sales about a custom subscription based on your expected accounts, transfer volumes, and refresh frequency.

Does the estimate include everything on my final bill?

It estimates planned LC Bridge base usage in USD. Applicable account markup and tax can change the final amount. Authorized production rates and billing terms govern actual charges.
