Where nobody else is

Everyone else is somewhere your CRM isn't.

AI in communities isn't new. Circle's agents comment in-feed; HighLevel's own AI schedules posts. But look at what each one is actually connected to. An agent that can post but has never seen the contact record can't tell you who's ready to buy — and one that lives in another platform can't fire on your workflows. The line we won't cross

Amplify humans. Never impersonate them.

Every agent is named as AI, badged as AI, and explains itself in a pinned post. Your members will know it's AI — and they'll still show up, because someone answered them at 7am.

No invented members. No fabricated wins, testimonials, results or member facts. Ever.
Humans answer first. On non-urgent questions the agent waits, so your members get to help each other.
The offer comes from you. Agents spot intent and tell you. They never pitch in public.
Sensitive things escalate. Refunds, complaints, health and safety go to you — never to a public reply.

This isn't only principle. The FTC's rule on fake reviews and testimonials, effective October 2024, carries civil penalties for AI-generated endorsements from people who don't exist. Disclosure isn't a constraint on the product — it's the reason it's safe to run.

Before you ask

The questions everyone asks first.

Will my members know they're talking to AI?

Yes, and that's deliberate. Every agent has AI in its name, an AI badge on its avatar, and a pinned post explaining what it is. The first time an agent interacts with any member it says so in a line. Members tolerate — and increasingly expect — a labelled AI helper. What they punish is impersonation.

Does this work with Skool or Circle?

No. Community Agents is built for HighLevel Communities specifically, because that's where we can reach both the feed and the CRM record behind every member. Skool has no public API, and Circle's data stays inside Circle. Depth is the whole point — a shallow version of this on four platforms would be worse than a deep version on one.

Will it post things I wouldn't say?

Not unless you let it. Every action starts on Approve, so drafts come to you before they go out; you loosen it action by action as you get comfortable. Quill in particular is trained on your own posts, so what it drafts already sounds like you — and it never publishes without approval by default.

Isn't fake engagement against the rules?

It is, and we don't do it. The FTC's rule on fake reviews and testimonials makes AI-generated endorsements from non-existent people a civil penalty matter. Our agents never pose as members, never fabricate wins or testimonials, and never inflate anything to look popular. They amplify things real people actually said.

What does it cost?

Not set yet — that's part of what we're working out with the first group. LC Bridge prices per active sub-account rather than per seat, and Community Agents will follow the same shape. Join the waitlist and you'll hear the number before anyone else.
